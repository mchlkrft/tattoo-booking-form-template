# Tattoo Booking Reply Templates

A structured request only saves time if the reply is just as clear. This guide gives tattoo artists copy-paste messages for the main booking decisions: ask for missing details, approve a request, send a deposit request, release an expired hold, confirm the appointment, move a request to the waitlist, decline it, or handle a reschedule.

Use these as starting points. Replace every `___` placeholder, match the wording to your own voice, and keep the final decision outside Instagram DMs when possible so the request and reply stay together.

## The reply sequence

Most tattoo requests need only a few steps:

1. Review the idea, placement, size, references, timing, and budget.
2. Ask for missing details if you cannot make a decision yet.
3. Approve, waitlist, or decline the request.
4. For an approval, send the quote, deposit amount, policy, and payment deadline.
5. Confirm the date only after the deposit arrives.

An approval is not the same as a confirmed appointment. Say which stage the client is in so nobody assumes a date is reserved before the deposit is paid.

## Ask for missing details

Use this when the request is promising but incomplete. Ask only for the details you actually need.

```text
Hey ___, thanks for sending this through. I like the direction, but I need a
little more information before I can review it properly:

- ___
- ___
- ___

Please reply through ___ by ___. Once I have that, I can confirm whether the
piece fits my books and send the next steps.
```

For missing reference images, be specific about what would help: visual mood, subject, placement photo, or a clear photo of an existing tattoo for a cover-up.

## Approve a tattoo request

This message says yes to the project, not yet to a final appointment.

```text
Hey ___, I would be happy to take this on. The idea, placement, and size fit my
work.

Suggested plan: ___
Estimated session length: ___
Estimated price or range: ___
Possible dates: ___

To reserve a date, the deposit is ___ and the deposit policy is here: ___.
Choose your preferred date through ___ and I will send the payment step.
```

Use a range when the final time depends on detail, placement, or how the session moves. State what could change the estimate instead of pretending the first number is exact.

## Send a deposit request

Keep the amount, deadline, selected date, and policy in the same message.

```text
Your request is approved and I can hold ___ at ___ for you until ___.

Deposit: ___
Payment link or method: ___
Deposit policy: ___

The appointment is confirmed when the deposit arrives. If payment is not
received by the deadline, I may release the date to another client.
```

Never ask a client to send card details through email or DMs. Use your normal secure payment method.

## Release an expired deposit hold

Use this after the stated deadline passes and you have checked that the payment did not arrive. Do not call the booking canceled if it was never confirmed.

```text
Hey ___, the deposit deadline for the appointment hold on ___ has passed and I
have not received the payment, so the date is no longer on hold.

If you still want to book, reply through ___. I can check current availability,
but I cannot promise the same date.
```

If a late payment arrives after the date was released, check the payment and your current availability before promising a replacement date. Record it as unmatched until the next step is agreed.

## Confirm the appointment

Send this only after the deposit is received.

```text
You are booked.

Date and time: ___
Studio and address: ___
Deposit received: ___
Estimated remaining balance: ___
What to bring or prepare: ___
Deposit and rescheduling policy: ___

If anything changes, contact me through ___ at least ___ before the
appointment. I will send a reminder closer to the date.
```

For a guest spot, repeat the city and studio even if they already appeared in the request. Travel bookings are easier to misread when several cities are open at once.

## Move a request to the waitlist

A useful waitlist message explains why the request is not booked, what the list means, and whether the client needs to do anything.

```text
Hey ___, I like this request, but I cannot fit it into the current booking
window for ___.

I can add it to the waitlist for ___ instead. This is not a confirmed
appointment and no payment is needed. If a suitable date opens, I will contact
you through ___ before offering it publicly.

Your request will stay on the list until ___. Let me know if you book elsewhere
or no longer want the spot.
```

Do not call a decline a waitlist unless you would genuinely offer the client a future slot.

## Decline a request

A decline can be short and respectful. You do not owe a long defense, and you should not leave room for negotiation when the fit is wrong.

```text
Hey ___, thanks for trusting me with the idea. I am going to pass on this
request because ___.

I do not think I am the right artist for this piece, and I would rather be
honest than take on work that does not fit my direction. No payment has been
taken and no appointment was reserved.

I hope you find the right artist for it.
```

Useful, honest reasons include style fit, project scope, placement, schedule, or the request being outside your current booking criteria. Avoid judging the client or the idea.

If you know a suitable artist and have their permission to refer work, you can add one recommendation. Do not promise availability on another artist's behalf.

## Decline because books are full

Use this when the project fits but capacity does not.

```text
Hey ___, thanks for the request. My current booking window is full, so I cannot
offer a date for this one.

My books are expected to reopen around ___, or you can join the waitlist here:
___. The waitlist is not a confirmed booking and no payment is taken.
```

## Reschedule an appointment

Restate the original date, the policy, and the next action. Do not make the client reconstruct the booking from an old thread.

```text
I have your request to move the appointment from ___.

Under the rescheduling policy, ___ applies to the current deposit. The dates I
can offer instead are:

- ___
- ___
- ___

Reply through ___ by ___ with your preferred option. The new date is confirmed
when I send the updated confirmation.
```

If the artist needs to reschedule, take responsibility, give realistic alternatives, and state clearly whether the client can choose a refund under the applicable policy and local law.

## Cancel an appointment

```text
I have canceled the appointment on ___ at ___.

Deposit outcome under the agreed policy: ___
Any remaining refund or credit: ___
Expected processing time, if applicable: ___

No further appointment is reserved. If you want to request a new project later,
use ___.
```

## Keep every reply scannable

A client should be able to find the decision and next action without rereading a long conversation.

- Put the decision in the first two lines
- Include the project or guest spot context
- Use exact dates, times, amounts, and deadlines
- Link the policy instead of paraphrasing it differently each time
- Say whether the appointment is held, approved, waitlisted, declined, or confirmed
- Give one clear next action
- Keep sensitive health details out of routine booking replies

## Booking reply checklist

- [ ] The booking state is explicit
- [ ] The next action belongs to one person
- [ ] Dates include the city or studio when travel is involved
- [ ] Price wording distinguishes an estimate from a fixed quote
- [ ] Deposit amount, deadline, and policy are together
- [ ] An expired deposit hold is released only after the payment record is checked
- [ ] Confirmation is sent only after the deposit arrives
- [ ] Waitlist wording does not imply a guaranteed appointment
- [ ] Declines are direct, respectful, and free of fake excuses
- [ ] No card details, private health notes, or internal system details appear in the message
