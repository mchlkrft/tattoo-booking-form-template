# Tattoo Booking Request Review Checklist

Intended reader: tattoo artists reviewing incoming custom, flash, local, or travel requests.

A booking form collects the details. The review is where the artist decides whether those details add up to a tattoo they can actually take on.

This checklist starts after a request arrives. It does not repeat every field that belongs on the form. For that, use the [tattoo booking form fields](tattoo-booking-form-fields.md) guide. Use this page to review each request in the same order, make a clear decision, and stop promising dates before the project has been checked.

## First pass: is the request complete enough to review?

You do not need every possible detail. You need enough to judge fit, scope, and timing.

- **Idea:** The client explains the subject and direction in their own words.
- **Placement:** The body area and side are clear enough to understand the canvas.
- **Size:** There is a rough measurement or a useful comparison such as palm-sized.
- **References:** Images show mood, composition, subject, or style direction. They are not a request to copy another tattoo line for line.
- **Timing:** The client gives a window or explains any fixed date.
- **Contact:** You have a reliable way to send the decision and next step.
- **Location:** For travel work, the request names the correct city or guest spot.

If one missing answer would change your decision, ask for it. If it would not, keep moving.

## Second pass: does the project fit?

Review the tattoo before you review the calendar.

### Style fit

Is this work you want to make, and does it fit the direction clients can reasonably expect from your portfolio? A familiar subject can still be the wrong fit if the requested finish is outside your work.

### Scope

Can the piece be completed in one session, or does it need a larger project plan? Check whether the requested size supports the detail, whether the placement changes the drawing, and whether multiple sittings are realistic.

### Placement

Look for information that affects the design or session: an existing tattoo, scar tissue, a difficult wrap, or a placement photo you still need. Do not diagnose skin from a booking form. If there is a health or healing concern, pause and ask the client to follow appropriate professional advice.

### Timing and location

Does the client's date window match your books? For a guest spot, confirm the city and trip dates before discussing a slot.

### Price context

Do you have enough information to give a fixed quote, a range, or a consultation-first answer? Do not let an early budget number force a blind quote. The [tattoo pricing and quote requests](tattoo-pricing-and-quotes-guide.md) guide covers that step.

## Give the request one clear status

Every reviewed request should leave your hands with one status and one next action.

| Status | What it means | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| Needs details | The idea may fit, but a decision would be a guess | Ask only for the missing information |
| Consultation first | The project needs a real conversation before approval | Offer a consultation format and deadline |
| Approved | You want to take the project and can discuss dates | Send the plan, estimate, dates, and deposit step |
| Waitlisted | The project fits, but the current window does not | Explain what the list means and when you may follow up |
| Declined | The project, timing, or scope does not fit | Say no clearly and close the request |

Approved does not mean booked. The appointment becomes confirmed only after the client completes the steps in your policy, usually including the deposit.

## A quick review routine

1. Read the full request once without replying.
2. Check the idea, placement, size, references, timing, contact, and city where relevant.
3. Write down the one thing that blocks a decision, if there is one.
4. Decide the status before opening the calendar.
5. Send one reply with the decision and one next action.
6. Record the status where you keep the request.

Batching this work can help. Review a set of requests at a planned time, then handle deposits and calendar confirmation as a separate step. That keeps the first pass focused on the tattoo instead of whichever date looks easiest to fill.

## Internal review note

Copy this into a private note, spreadsheet, or booking record:

```text
Client: ___
Project: ___
Placement and size: ___
City or studio: ___

Style fit: Yes / No / Unsure
Scope: One session / Multi-session / Consultation first
Missing detail: ___
Timing fit: Yes / No / Waitlist
Estimate or quote approach: ___

Decision: Needs details / Consultation / Approved / Waitlisted / Declined
Next action: ___
Reply deadline: ___
```

Keep health details and candid internal notes out of client-facing replies. Store only what you actually need, with access limited to the people who need it.

## Review checklist

- [ ] The idea is understandable without reopening a DM thread
- [ ] Placement and rough size are clear
- [ ] References show direction without asking for a direct copy
- [ ] Timing and city match the booking context
- [ ] Style and scope fit the work you take on
- [ ] Any missing detail would genuinely change the decision
- [ ] The request has one status
- [ ] The reply gives one next action
- [ ] No date is called confirmed before the confirmation steps are complete
- [ ] The status is recorded outside your memory
