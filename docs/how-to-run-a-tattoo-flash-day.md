# How to Run a Tattoo Flash Day

Intended reader: tattoo artists organizing a flash day, whether it runs by appointment, walk-in, or a mix of both.

A flash day looks simple from the outside: post the sheet, fill the slots, tattoo all day. The mess usually starts between those steps. The same design gets claimed by three people, screenshots lose their labels, deposits arrive without names, and a late first session pushes the whole day sideways.

A clean flash day gives every design an identity, every request a status, and every confirmed client a time and prep message. The art can stay spontaneous. The booking flow cannot.

## Choose the format first

Pick one format and say it clearly.

### Appointment only

Clients request a design in advance. You approve the request, take any required deposit, and assign a time. This works best when the tattoos vary in size or placement.

### Walk-in only

Clients arrive and choose from what is still available. You need a visible queue, a firm capacity limit, and a clear cutoff when the day is full.

### Hybrid

Some designs or slots are reserved in advance, while the rest stay open for walk-ins. Mark the two pools clearly so a walk-in design is not accidentally promised online.

Do not advertise all three at once without explaining which designs and times belong to each format.

## Build a sheet people can claim without guessing

Give every design a short ID or unique name. The ID should appear on the master sheet, the booking request, the confirmation, and your day-of list.

Publish the practical rules with the art:

- Whether each design can be tattooed once or repeated
- Fixed size, size range, or allowed adjustments
- Placement limits, if any
- Color, black and grey, or fixed palette
- Price or starting price
- Whether the deposit applies to the final price
- Flash day date, city, and studio
- How to request a design
- When a claim becomes confirmed

Keep one master version of the sheet. If a design is claimed, mark that version instead of posting a second crop that nobody can match to the first.

## Set capacity around real session blocks

Work backward from the hours you can tattoo well.

Include time for:

- Client arrival and paperwork
- Placement and stencil changes
- Setup and cleaning between clients
- Photos and payment
- Food and short breaks
- One late arrival or session that runs long

Then decide which designs fit which blocks. A detailed rib piece and a small forearm flash are not interchangeable just because both came from the same sheet.

## Use a request flow, not a comment race

Comments and DMs are useful for attention, but weak as the final claim record. Ask each serious client to send:

- Primary design ID
- One or two backup choices
- Placement and rough size
- Preferred time blocks
- Flexibility across the day
- Name and contact details
- Deposit policy acknowledgment, if used

The [flash day request template](../templates/flash-day-request-template.md) is ready to copy into a form.

## Give every claim a status

Use the same status words everywhere.

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Available | Nobody has an active hold or confirmed booking |
| Requested | A client asked for it, but you have not approved the fit |
| Held | You approved it and are waiting for the deposit or other confirmation step |
| Confirmed | The client completed the required step and has a time |
| Released | The hold expired and the design is available again |
| Completed | The tattoo was done |

A comment saying "mine" is a request, not a confirmation. Make that rule public before the sheet drops.

## Approve, hold, then confirm

1. Review the design, placement, size, and time preference.
2. Approve the request if the placement and block make sense.
3. Offer a specific time and state how long the hold lasts.
4. Send the deposit amount, payment method, and policy where applicable.
5. Confirm only after the required step is complete.
6. Release the design or time if the deadline passes.
7. Offer a backup choice if the primary design is already confirmed.

This keeps the first person to type from automatically becoming the wrong booking for the piece.

## Publish updates from one source

Choose one public place where clients can check availability. It might be an updated post, a story highlight, or a page linked from your bio.

Use simple labels:

- Available
- On hold until ___
- Claimed

Do not expose client names, deposits, or private request details on the public sheet.

## Send the day-of details early

A few days before the event, send every confirmed client:

- Date and exact arrival time
- Studio name, address, and access instructions
- Design ID, placement, and agreed size
- Deposit received and estimated balance
- Payment methods accepted on the day
- What to wear or bring for the placement
- Guest, lateness, reschedule, and cancellation rules
- The best contact method if travel goes wrong

Use the pre-appointment client message template and adjust it for the shorter flash-day pace.

## Run the room from a private day list

Keep one list in appointment order:

```text
Time: ___
Client: ___
Design ID: ___
Placement and size: ___
Deposit: Requested / Received / Not used
Balance and payment method: ___
Consent or studio paperwork: ___
Notes: ___
Status: Expected / Arrived / In session / Complete / No-show
```

The public sheet answers "what is left?" The private list answers "what happens next?" Do not make one document carry both jobs.

## After the flash day

- Mark completed and still-available designs
- Reconcile deposits and balances
- Send aftercare and follow-up information
- Ask before re-releasing a design that had an unusual client-led adjustment
- Save clear photos with the matching design ID
- Note which session blocks were too tight or too loose
- Decide whether unclaimed work stays available, retires, or moves to the next event

## Flash day checklist

- [ ] Format is appointment, walk-in, or clearly explained hybrid
- [ ] Every design has a unique ID
- [ ] Repeat, size, placement, color, and price rules are public
- [ ] Capacity includes cleaning, breaks, and delay buffer
- [ ] Claims use a structured request, not only comments or DMs
- [ ] Requested, held, and confirmed mean different things
- [ ] Holds have a deadline
- [ ] One public source shows current availability
- [ ] Confirmed clients receive time, address, prep, and policy details
- [ ] A private day list runs the schedule
- [ ] Payments, follow-ups, and remaining designs are closed out afterward
