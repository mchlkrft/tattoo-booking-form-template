# Tattoo Booking Form Fields

A tattoo booking form is only as useful as the questions you ask. This guide covers which fields a freelance or traveling tattoo artist should collect on a tattoo intake form, why each one matters, and which are essential, optional, or situational.

The default rule: collect enough context for the artist to make a real decision before the calendar opens.

## Essential fields

These should appear on every tattoo booking request.

### Name

A first name or preferred handle is enough. You do not need a legal name on intake.

### Email

Used for confirmation, magic links, deposit instructions, and rescheduling. Phone numbers can substitute, but email is the lowest-friction default.

### Tattoo idea

Free text. Let the client describe what they want in their own words. The most useful field on the entire form. Read it carefully before anything else.

### Placement

The body area. Forearm, ribs, back of calf. Placement affects time, pain, healing, and pricing. Without it, you cannot price or schedule properly.

### Approximate size

A rough size, in centimeters or in plain language ("palm sized", "from wrist to elbow"). Combined with placement, size drives most of the scope conversation.

### Reference images

At least one. Reference images keep style direction visual rather than verbal. Allow multiple uploads.

### Deposit acknowledgment

A checkbox confirming the client has read your deposit policy. Even if no deposit is taken yet, this sets expectations. See [tattoo-deposit-policy-template.md](tattoo-deposit-policy-template.md).

### Age confirmation

In most regions, 18 is the minimum age for tattoos. Add a confirmation checkbox where local laws require it.

## Recommended fields

These help most artists but are not strictly required.

### Instagram handle

Adds context. You can check the client's profile to see whether they have other tattoos, what style they gravitate to, and whether they have followed you for a while.

### Preferred dates

A date window works better than a single date. "Late March or early April" gives both sides flexibility.

### Style

Realism, blackwork, fineline, traditional, neotraditional, ornamental. Helps you decide whether the request fits your portfolio.

### Phone number

Useful for last-minute schedule changes, but not required on intake.

## Situational fields

Only ask when relevant. Adding fields that do not apply slows down every client who fills out the form.

### Existing tattoos and cover-up info

For cover-ups, the client should attach photos of the existing tattoo and note its age, size, and ink color.

### Skin notes

Scars, recent surgery, sensitive areas. Worth asking when placement is on the ribs, sternum, hands, neck, or near scars.

### City, studio, or guest spot

Required for guest spots. Optional for home-studio bookings. See [guest-spot-booking-workflow.md](guest-spot-booking-workflow.md) for the full traveling-artist setup.

### Budget range

Filters out clients whose budget will not match the work. Optional but useful for larger pieces.

## Field priority checklist

- [ ] Name
- [ ] Email
- [ ] Tattoo idea
- [ ] Placement
- [ ] Approximate size
- [ ] Reference images
- [ ] Deposit acknowledgment
- [ ] Age confirmation (where required)
- [ ] Preferred dates
- [ ] Style preference
- [ ] City or guest spot (when traveling)

## What to leave off

A tattoo booking form is not a tax return. Do not ask for:

- Full legal address on intake
- Date of birth (use an age checkbox instead)
- Government ID
- Social media follower count
- Long open-ended questionnaires about lifestyle

If the request gets approved, you can collect anything else you need afterward.
