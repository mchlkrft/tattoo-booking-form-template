# Tattoo Aftercare Follow-Up and Touch-Up Requests

The booking form gets all the attention, but the client relationship does not end when the needle stops. Healing questions, touch-up requests, healed photos, reviews, and rebookings all happen after the session, and most artists handle none of it on purpose. So it scatters back into the same Instagram DMs the booking form was meant to replace. This guide covers the post-session stage: a simple follow-up timeline, copy-paste aftercare and check-in messages, a fair touch-up policy, and a structured touch-up request form so returning work arrives with the same clarity as a first booking. It pairs with the [client intake template](../templates/tattoo-client-intake-template.md) and the [deposit policy template](tattoo-deposit-policy-template.md), and it works with any tool.

A quick note on scope: these are workflow templates, not aftercare instructions. Follow your own healing guidance and local health advice for the actual aftercare content, and treat healing times here as rough averages that vary by person and placement.

## Why the post-session stage is worth a system

Booking is only the front half of the relationship. The back half is where retention, portfolio, and reputation come from, and it is the part most artists leave to chance.

- A short healing check catches a problem early, while it is still fixable
- A touch-up request is a booking request, so it deserves the same structure, not a one-line DM
- Healed photos in good light are your best portfolio and your best marketing
- A follow-up is the natural moment to invite a review and the next booking
- A returning client costs you far less attention than finding a new one
- Silence sends every healing question and touch-up ask back into scattered DMs

## Aftercare follow-up timeline

You do not need many touchpoints. A few well-timed messages cover almost every piece. Scale up for large or complex work, scale down for small pieces.

| When | Message | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Day of or day after | Aftercare recap and one line on when to reach out | Client has the healing info in writing, not just verbally |
| 2 to 3 days | Optional quick check for large or complex pieces | Catch early irritation on bigger work |
| Around 2 weeks | Surface-healed check-in | Confirm the outer skin settled and answer questions |
| 4 to 6 weeks | Healed photo request, touch-up window, review and rebook | Fully healed is the right moment for photos and any fix |

The last touchpoint does the most work: it gets you a healed photo, opens the touch-up window on your terms, and invites the review and the next booking, all at once.

## Copy-paste follow-up messages

Keep them short and human. Paste these into your email tool, DM auto-reply, or booking tool and swap in your own aftercare wording. Fill the blanks marked `___`.

```markdown
## 1. Aftercare recap (day of or day after)

Hey ___, thanks again for today. Quick recap so it heals clean:

[your aftercare steps here in short bullets]

Keep it simple and do not overthink it. If anything looks off, message me a
photo rather than guessing. I will check in with you in a couple of weeks.


## 2. Two-week check-in

Hey ___, hope the ___ is settling in nicely. How is it healing? Send a photo
if you have any questions. Once it is fully healed in a few weeks I will ask
for a proper photo for the portfolio.


## 3. Healed photo, touch-up, review and rebook (4 to 6 weeks)

Hey ___, your ___ should be fully healed now. Three quick things:

1. If any spots healed light or patchy, I offer a touch-up. Send a healed
   photo in good daylight and I will take a look. My touch-up policy is here: ___
2. If you are happy with it, a photo in good light really helps my portfolio,
   and a review means a lot: ___
3. Thinking about the next one? My books and how to request are here: ___


## 4. Touch-up follow-up (only if a touch-up is needed)

Thanks for the photo. That looks like a normal ___ and a quick fix. Send a
touch-up request with your availability here: ___ and we will get it sorted.
```

## What counts as a touch-up

Being clear about this up front prevents most awkward conversations later.

- A touch-up is minor work on a healed tattoo: patchy areas, light spots, small gaps, or lines that dropped ink during healing.
- A touch-up is not a redesign, an addition, a size increase, or a color change. That is a new session with a new request and its own quote.

If the client wants to change or add to the piece, route them back to a normal [booking request](../templates/tattoo-client-intake-template.md), not a touch-up.

## A fair touch-up policy

Your policy is yours to set. What matters is that it exists in writing and the client agrees to it before booking, the same way they agree to the deposit policy. A common shape:

| Usually covered | Usually not covered |
|---|---|
| Patchy healing or minor ink loss on your own work | Damage from skipped or poor aftercare |
| Small fixes within your stated window | Sun damage, picking, or scabbing that pulled ink out |
| Line or spot that dropped during healing | Additions, changes, or a bigger piece |
| Normal settling on most placements | High-wear areas often excluded or charged (hands, feet, fingers, lips, inner ear) |
| | Another artist's work |

Decisions to make explicit in your policy:

- The free window. Many artists offer a free or discounted touch-up within a set window after the tattoo is fully healed, commonly somewhere in the 2 to 6 month range. After that it is a paid session.
- Whether a deposit applies to the touch-up booking, and whether it is waived for a genuine fix on your work.
- What voids it. Aftercare neglect, sun damage, and picking are the usual exclusions.
- High-wear placements. State up front which areas you touch up for free, which are paid, and which you do not guarantee.

Put the policy where the deposit policy lives, and link it from the booking form so it is agreed before the appointment, not argued after.

## Touch-up request fields

When a touch-up is genuinely needed, collect it as a structured request, not a DM thread. This keeps it tied to the original booking and gives you what you need to schedule quickly. Paste this into any form builder.

```markdown
## Touch-up request

Name or handle: ___
Email: ___
Original tattoo (what it is): ___
Roughly when was it done: ___
Was it my work, or another artist's: ___
Placement: ___
What needs attention (light spots, gaps, lines): ___
Healed photos in good daylight: [attach or link]

- [ ] The tattoo is fully healed (at least a few weeks old)
- [ ] I have followed the aftercare instructions
- [ ] I have read the touch-up policy and understand what is covered

Preferred dates or window: ___
Anything else I should know: ___
```

## Touch-ups on another artist's work

Requests to fix or refresh another artist's tattoo come up, especially for traveling and guest spot artists. Treat these as a new booking, not a warranty. You cannot vouch for the original linework, ink, or how it was healed, so price and schedule it like fresh work, ask for clear healed photos first, and be honest about what is and is not possible. Some artists decline these entirely, which is a fine policy as long as it is stated.

## Common mistakes

- **No follow-up at all.** The healing questions still come. They just come as messy DMs instead of a clean check-in you controlled.
- **Treating a touch-up like a casual DM.** Without a structured request you lose the original context and end up asking the same questions again.
- **An unwritten touch-up policy.** If it is not written down and agreed before booking, every touch-up becomes a negotiation.
- **Promising a free touch-up with no window or conditions.** Open-ended free work invites requests years later and for damage that was not your fault.
- **Forgetting to ask for a healed photo.** You lose the portfolio shot and the record of how the piece actually settled.
- **Touching up before the tattoo is fully healed.** Working too early misreads normal healing as a flaw and can cause more damage.

## Post-session follow-up checklist

- [ ] Aftercare recap sent in writing on the day or the day after
- [ ] Follow-up timing decided (at least a healed check around 4 to 6 weeks)
- [ ] Touch-up policy written and shared before the client books
- [ ] Free window, conditions, and excluded placements stated
- [ ] Touch-up requests collected through a structured form, not DMs
- [ ] Healed photo requested in good daylight
- [ ] Review and next booking invited at the fully-healed moment
- [ ] Returning client details kept with their original booking record
