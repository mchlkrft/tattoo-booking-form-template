# Tattoo Consent and Health Intake Fields

Most artists either skip health questions to keep the booking form short, or bury them inside a dense day-of waiver the client signs without reading. Neither helps you tattoo safely. This guide covers the consent and health fields worth collecting at the booking or request stage, why each one matters, and how the booking-stage pre-screen differs from the signed legal waiver you complete in person. It pairs with the [booking form fields](tattoo-booking-form-fields.md) reference and the [client intake template](../templates/tattoo-client-intake-template.md), and it ends with a copy-paste Markdown addendum you can bolt onto either.

The whole point of asking at booking is timing: a light health pre-screen on the request form lets you flag a contraindication and reschedule before the client travels to your chair. That matters for everyone, and it is load-bearing for guest spot and traveling artists.

## Important disclaimer

This guide does not constitute legal or medical advice. Consent, health-data collection, and minimum-age rules are governed by local health regulations, consumer and medical law, and data-protection law (GDPR where it applies), so have a local lawyer or your studio manager review your wording, and check your local health authority for the day-of consent form and waiver requirements before you publish anything. The fields below are a practical starting point for the intake stage, not a regulatory checklist.

## Consent versus health intake versus the legal waiver

These three get mixed together, which is why forms end up bloated or unsafe. They are separate jobs:

- Consent acknowledgment at intake: a few checkboxes confirming the client understands the process (a tattoo is permanent, they consent to being tattooed, they have read the deposit policy). This is not the signed waiver.
- Health intake: the medical, medication, allergy, and skin context you need to tattoo safely and to spot a reason to pause.
- The legal waiver: the dated, signed liability document, completed in person on the day, along with ID and age verification. That is out of scope here. Defer its wording to local law and a lawyer.

This doc covers the first two: the consent acknowledgments and the health pre-screen that belong on the booking or request form.

## What to collect at booking versus on the day

A common mistake is trying to do everything on one form. Split it. Collect a light pre-screen at booking so nobody travels for an appointment that should have been rescheduled, and keep the legally binding parts for in person.

| At booking (the form) | On the day (in person) |
|---|---|
| Light health pre-screen (yes/no flags) | Full medical sign-off and any follow-up questions |
| Contraindication flags worth a conversation | The dated, signed consent form and liability waiver |
| Consent acknowledgments (process, deposit, aftercare) | Photo ID and age verification |
| Photography and portfolio consent | Wet or e-signature on the waiver |

The booking-stage pre-screen is a filter, not a medical record. It tells you whether to confirm, ask a follow-up, or reschedule.

## Health and medical fields to collect

Ask these as plain yes/no flags with a short reason, not a medical interrogation. You are screening for things that change bleeding, healing, or safety.

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Diabetes or a healing disorder | Slower healing and higher infection risk |
| Bleeding or clotting disorder (e.g. haemophilia) | Affects bleeding during the session |
| Heart condition | May need a doctor's go-ahead first |
| Epilepsy or seizures | Safety planning for a long session |
| Immune-compromising condition | Healing and infection risk |
| Faints easily, or first tattoo | Plan breaks, food, and positioning |

## Medications to ask about

Medications matter for the same reasons: bleeding, skin integrity, and how the tattoo heals. Ask whether the client currently takes or recently finished any of these.

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Blood thinners or anticoagulants | Increased bleeding, harder to work cleanly |
| Isotretinoin (Accutane) or recent acne medication | Thins and sensitizes skin, common wait period after stopping |
| Steroids | Affect skin and healing |
| Recent antibiotics | Often a sign of a recent or ongoing infection |
| Immunosuppressants | Slower healing, higher infection risk |

You are not diagnosing. A yes is a prompt to ask a follow-up or suggest the client check with their doctor, not an automatic no.

## Allergies to disclose

A reaction during or after a session is avoidable when you know in advance.

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Tattoo ink and pigments | Pigment allergies, red is a common culprit |
| Latex | Glove and barrier contact |
| Antiseptics and soaps | Prep and cleaning products |
| Adhesives and bandages | Aftercare wrap reactions |
| Nickel and metals | Relevant for jewellery and some equipment contact |

## Skin conditions at the placement

The general skin notes (scars, recent surgery, sensitive areas) already live in the [booking form fields](tattoo-booking-form-fields.md) reference. The point here is conditions at or near where the tattoo goes, because an active flare is usually a reschedule.

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Eczema or psoriasis at the site | Tattooing over an active flare is risky, often postponed |
| Moles in the area | Artists usually tattoo around them, not over |
| Keloid or scarring tendency | Affects how the skin takes and heals |
| Recent or active irritation, sunburn, or rash | Wait for the skin to settle first |

When a condition is active at the placement, the safe answer is usually to reschedule, not to refuse outright.

## Pregnancy and breastfeeding

Ask one question: is the client pregnant or breastfeeding. Many artists decline or postpone in that case as a matter of policy and caution. Frame it as your policy and a safety call, and leave the medical specifics to the client and their doctor and to local guidance.

## Recent and day-of factors

Some things change between booking and the appointment, so note which you flag on the form and which you confirm in person.

- Recent sunburn or heavy tanning on the area (flag at booking, confirm on the day)
- Recent surgery or procedure on or near the site (flag at booking)
- Current illness or fever (confirm on the day)
- Alcohol or drugs: a client who is intoxicated cannot give valid consent and cannot be tattooed (confirm on the day)
- Eating beforehand: a reminder, not a screen, but it cuts down on fainting

## Bloodborne and transmittable conditions, asked respectfully

You can ask about bloodborne conditions without making it an interrogation. The reason you ask is that your safety protocol and universal precautions apply to everyone the same way, not so you can refuse someone. Say that on the form, and add a line that answers are kept private and that being honest keeps both of you safe.

## Consent and acknowledgment fields

These are the acknowledgments that belong at intake. They are confirmations, not the signed in-person waiver.

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Understands a tattoo is permanent | Sets expectations up front |
| Consents to the tattooing process and needles | Basic informed consent at the request stage |
| Has read the deposit policy | Ties to your [deposit policy](tattoo-deposit-policy-template.md) |
| Will arrive fit to be tattooed | Not unwell, not under the influence |
| Aftercare instructions received | Confirms the client has the healing info |
| Photography, portfolio, and social media use | Consent to use images, a data-protection point |
| Age confirmation | Confirms the client meets the local minimum age |

## Region notes

Rules are local and they change, so treat this as a prompt to check, not a ruling. A few patterns worth knowing:

- There is often no single national rule. In the US, tattoo regulation sits with state and county health departments. The UK and EU vary by country.
- Minimum age and parental or guardian consent rules differ. Eighteen is common, some regions allow 16 to 17 with guardian consent, and some ban tattooing minors entirely.
- Some regions require a specific consent form or health questionnaire by regulation, and some set record-retention periods (commonly somewhere in the 1 to 7 year range).
- Under GDPR and UK GDPR, health information and identifiable photos are sensitive personal data, which raises the bar for how you collect and store them.

Check your local health authority and a lawyer for the specifics that apply where you work, and for every city you travel to.

## Honesty, privacy, and non-judgment

Clients under-disclose when a form feels like a test. A short line at the top of the health section fixes most of that. Tell them three things: you ask for safety, not to judge; withholding something raises the risk for both of you; and their answers are kept private and only used to tattoo them safely. That framing gets you more honest answers than a wall of medical checkboxes ever will.

## Data handling and storage

Health answers and reference photos are sensitive, so handle them like it.

- Collect only what you will actually act on
- State on the form why you collect it and how it is used
- Restrict who can see the responses
- Set a habit for retention and deletion rather than keeping everything forever
- Never leave health details or photos sitting in a public DM thread or a shared spreadsheet

## Health intake addendum you can copy

Append this to the [client intake template](../templates/tattoo-client-intake-template.md) or paste it into any form builder, CRM, or Notion page. It is the booking-stage pre-screen only. The signed consent form, waiver, and ID and age check are completed in person.

```markdown
## Health and consent (pre-screen)

These questions help your artist tattoo you safely. Answers are kept private and
are only used to plan your session. If you are unsure about anything, add it to
the notes line. Final consent, ID, and age check happen in person on the day.

Check anything that applies to you:

- [ ] Diabetes or a condition that affects healing
- [ ] A bleeding or clotting disorder
- [ ] A heart condition
- [ ] Epilepsy or seizures
- [ ] A condition that affects your immune system
- [ ] I faint easily, or this is my first tattoo
- [ ] I take blood thinners or anticoagulants
- [ ] I am on, or recently finished, isotretinoin (Accutane) or acne medication
- [ ] I take steroids, recent antibiotics, or immunosuppressants
- [ ] Allergy to tattoo ink, latex, antiseptics, adhesives, or metals (nickel)
- [ ] A skin condition at the placement (eczema, psoriasis, keloids, irritation)
- [ ] Pregnant or breastfeeding
- [ ] Recent sunburn, surgery, or procedure on or near the area

Allergy details, if any: ___

Anything else your artist should know: ___

Acknowledgments:

- [ ] I understand a tattoo is permanent and I consent to the tattooing process
- [ ] I have read the deposit policy
- [ ] I will arrive fit to be tattooed (not unwell, not under the influence)
- [ ] I have received aftercare instructions
- [ ] I consent to photos of the tattoo being used in the portfolio and on social media
- [ ] I confirm I meet the minimum tattoo age for this location
```

## Common mistakes

- **Skipping health questions to keep the form short.** A two-line pre-screen prevents a wasted appointment and a safety problem.
- **Treating the intake checkbox as the signed waiver.** The acknowledgment confirms understanding. The dated, signed waiver still happens in person.
- **Collecting more medical detail than you will act on.** Ask what changes your decision, not everything possible.
- **Storing health info and photos in an open DM thread or spreadsheet.** Sensitive data needs limited access and a deletion habit.
- **Assuming one age rule covers every city you travel to.** Minimum age and consent rules vary by region, so check each one.

## Consent and health intake checklist

- [ ] Minimum-age confirmation present, pointing to the local rule
- [ ] Key health flags asked (conditions, medication and blood thinners, allergies, skin conditions, pregnancy)
- [ ] Placement skin notes asked
- [ ] Recent and day-of factors flagged
- [ ] Photography and portfolio consent captured
- [ ] Aftercare acknowledgment captured
- [ ] Form states why health data is collected and how it is stored
- [ ] Signed waiver, ID, and age check deferred to in person
- [ ] Wording reviewed against local law
