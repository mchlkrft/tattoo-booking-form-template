# Guest Spot Trip Planning Checklist

Intended reader: traveling tattoo artists planning a confirmed guest spot with a host studio.

A guest spot is half tattoo booking and half travel plan. Before the first request arrives, the artist and host studio need the city, dates, working setup, and client capacity to agree with each other.

The existing [guest spot booking workflow](guest-spot-booking-workflow.md) covers request intake and review. This companion checklist covers the trip around those requests: confirming the studio, setting realistic capacity, announcing the right information, preparing clients, and closing the trip cleanly.

## Confirm the host studio before announcing

Get the working arrangement in writing, even when you know the studio well.

- Studio name, address, and main contact
- Arrival, setup, working, and pack-down times
- Which days and hours you can tattoo
- Workstation, bed, lighting, and supplies provided by the studio
- Supplies and equipment you need to bring
- Cleaning, sterilization, consent, and waste procedures
- Rate, chair fee, commission, or other financial arrangement
- How clients enter the studio and who handles reception
- Which payment methods work in that country or city
- Local paperwork, insurance, licensing, or health requirements you need to verify

Rules vary by place. Check the host studio's process and the relevant local authority instead of assuming your home setup carries over.

## Build capacity from the trip, not from demand

Start with the time you can safely and realistically work. Do not start with the number of people asking in DMs.

1. Mark travel, setup, and pack-down time.
2. Block the studio hours available to you.
3. Add cleaning, drawing checks, food, and delay buffers.
4. Decide which session lengths can fit each day.
5. Keep at least one plan for a late cancellation or a piece that runs long.

The final number of appointments depends on your work, the placements, and the studio. A tiny flash trip and a backpiece consultation trip should not use the same schedule.

## Decide what fits this guest spot

A limited trip needs tighter project criteria than open local books. Write the criteria before reviewing requests.

- Styles and subjects you want to take
- Minimum or maximum practical size
- Placements you will or will not book on the trip
- One-session work versus projects that need a return visit
- Flash, custom work, or both
- Price or minimum information you will share publicly
- Whether local consultations are possible before the session

Clear criteria make approvals faster and declines more honest. They also stop a three-day guest spot from quietly turning into six unfinished projects.

## Set the booking window

Your announcement should state when requests open, when they close, and when clients can expect a decision.

Keep these dates together:

- Guest spot start and end
- Request opening date
- Request closing date
- Review period
- Deposit deadline for approved work
- Final confirmation or reminder date

Use a city-specific request path. A client asking for Berlin should not land in the same unlabelled pile as a home-studio request or a future Amsterdam trip.

## Announce enough context to act

Every public guest spot post, story, or booking page should make the next step obvious.

- City
- Studio, if confirmed and ready to publish
- Guest spot dates
- Types of work you want to book
- Request closing date
- One booking link
- A note that requests are reviewed before appointments are confirmed
- Waitlist direction if the trip fills

Avoid announcing with only a city sticker and "DM me." It creates attention, but no clean way to compare the actual requests.

## Review requests against the real trip

Check the tattoo and the travel context together.

1. Confirm the client selected the correct city and date window.
2. Review idea, placement, size, references, and contact details.
3. Check the project against your guest spot criteria and session blocks.
4. Approve the project before offering a date.
5. Send the estimate, deposit step, and deadline.
6. Confirm the appointment only after the required step is complete.
7. Move suitable overflow to the trip waitlist, with no promise of a slot.

The [tattoo booking request review checklist](tattoo-booking-request-review-checklist.md) gives a reusable first pass.

## Prepare the week before travel

- Reconfirm the studio address, contact, access, and working hours
- Recheck any documents, insurance, or local requirements
- Confirm every client has the date, time, studio address, and contact method
- Check which deposits have arrived and which holds expired
- Send the client prep message and studio arrival instructions
- Keep a private offline copy of the confirmed schedule and essential contacts
- Pack the equipment and supplies agreed with the studio
- Allow for transport delays between arrival and the first appointment

Do not keep the only copy of the trip schedule inside Instagram. Travel days are exactly when accounts, batteries, and connections become unreliable.

## Close the trip cleanly

After the last session:

- Reconcile appointments, deposits, balances, and studio fees
- Send aftercare and follow-up information
- Record any clients who need another session or touch-up review
- Mark waitlisted requests that still make sense for a return trip
- Note which city demand was real, which requests converted, and which project types fit the trip
- Thank the host studio and close any shared admin

The point is not to turn tattoo travel into office work. It is to keep the trip from living across screenshots, memory, and three different inboxes.

## Guest spot trip checklist

- [ ] Host studio and working arrangement are confirmed
- [ ] Local requirements have been checked
- [ ] Travel, setup, tattooing, breaks, and pack-down fit the dates
- [ ] Project criteria are written before requests are reviewed
- [ ] Booking window and decision timing are public
- [ ] Every announcement uses one city-specific booking link
- [ ] Requests are approved before dates are confirmed
- [ ] Deposits and expired holds are tracked
- [ ] Clients receive the address and prep message before travel
- [ ] A private schedule and essential contacts are available offline
- [ ] Waitlist demand is kept for a possible return trip
- [ ] Payments and studio fees are reconciled after the trip
