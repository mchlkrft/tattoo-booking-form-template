# Instagram DM Booking Workflow

Most tattoo artists get their first booking inquiries through Instagram DMs. That is not the problem. The problem is when DMs are also expected to carry the whole booking process: idea, references, placement, size, dates, deposit, confirmation. DMs were built for conversation, not for tattoo intake.

This guide explains why Instagram DM booking gets messy and how to move serious tattoo appointment requests into a cleaner structure without abandoning Instagram.

## Why DMs become messy for tattoo bookings

- The first message rarely contains everything you need.
- Details arrive across multiple replies, sometimes days apart.
- Reference images sit in one thread, the description in another.
- Story replies, reactions, and casual chat live in the same inbox as real booking requests.
- Closed books, deposits, and guest spots are hard to communicate from a chat thread.
- Artists ask the same intake questions every week.

A real booking decision needs context the artist can scan in one place. DMs scatter that context.

## Before and after

### Before: DM-only booking

1. Client sends a vague message asking about an idea or a price.
2. Artist replies asking for placement, size, references, dates.
3. Client answers one question at a time, across multiple replies.
4. Artist pieces the request together from memory.
5. Artist scrolls back through the thread to recheck details.
6. Approval, deposit, and confirmation also happen in the same thread.
7. The thread gets buried under newer messages.

### After: DM plus structured tattoo request form

1. Client sends a DM expressing interest.
2. Artist replies with one link: the booking request form.
3. Client submits a structured request with idea, placement, size, references, timing, and contact details.
4. Artist reviews the full request in one place.
5. Artist approves, declines, or moves the request to a waitlist.
6. Approval, deposit, and confirmation move out of DMs entirely.

DMs stay useful for trust, quick questions, and small talk. The booking link handles the structured part.

## What to put in your Instagram bio

Keep the link friction low. Two examples that work:

- "Booking requests through the link below."
- "For tattoos and guest spots, send your request here."

Skip the long pitch. The bio is one click away from the form.

## What to say in a DM reply

A reusable reply you can copy:

> Thanks for reaching out. To keep your request organized and make sure I have everything I need, please submit it through my booking link in my bio. I review every request personally.

That sentence covers the move from DM to structured request without sounding cold or automated.

## Things to keep in DMs

- Small talk and rapport
- Quick confirmations after a booking exists
- Last-minute updates close to an appointment
- Aftercare check-ins

## Things to move out of DMs

- Initial intake (idea, placement, size, references)
- Deposit collection
- Calendar coordination
- Long question threads about style, healing, pricing

## Checklist

- [ ] Booking link in Instagram bio
- [ ] Pinned story or highlight pointing to the booking link
- [ ] Saved reply that points clients to the booking link
- [ ] Booking form that collects idea, placement, size, references, dates, contact details
- [ ] A clear rule for yourself about what stays in DMs and what moves to the form
