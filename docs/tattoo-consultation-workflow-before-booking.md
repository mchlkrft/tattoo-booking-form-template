# Tattoo Consultation Workflow Before Booking

Intended reader: tattoo artists deciding whether a larger custom, cover-up, or multi-session request should move forward.

Some tattoos need a conversation before either side can commit. Large custom work, cover-ups, difficult placements, and multi-session projects often need more than a form. That does not mean the consultation should start from zero.

A useful consultation begins with a structured request, stays focused on the decisions that matter, and ends with a written next step. It is a bridge between interest and approval, not a vague chat that accidentally becomes a booking.

## Decide whether a consultation is needed

A consultation can help when:

- The project is large or likely to take multiple sessions
- Placement, scale, or body flow needs to be seen and discussed
- The tattoo is a cover-up, rework, or blast-over
- The client has several possible directions and needs help narrowing them
- References point in different directions
- The artist needs to explain technical limits before quoting
- Travel or guest spot timing affects whether the project can be completed

A consultation is usually unnecessary for straightforward flash, a clearly described small piece, or a repeatable project you already know how to scope from the request.

## Collect the basics before offering a call

Ask for enough context to decide whether a consultation is worth both people's time:

- Tattoo idea in the client's own words
- Placement and rough size
- Clear photos of the placement when relevant
- References with a note about what the client likes in each one
- Existing tattoo photos for cover-ups or reworks
- Preferred timing and city
- Budget context if you use it for project fit
- Contact details

Use the [tattoo client intake template](../templates/tattoo-client-intake-template.md) or [cover-up request template](../templates/cover-up-tattoo-request-template.md) rather than rebuilding the request inside a DM thread.

## Choose the lightest consultation format that works

Not every project needs an hour in person.

- **Written follow-up:** Best when one or two answers are missing.
- **Short video or phone call:** Useful for direction, scope, and travel questions.
- **In-person consultation:** Useful when placement, scale, movement, or existing work needs to be seen directly.

Tell the client the format, expected length, location or link, and what to prepare. If you charge for consultations, state the amount, what it covers, and whether it is credited later before the client agrees.

## Review the request before the consultation

Go in with a short list of decisions, not a blank page.

```text
Project: ___
What already makes sense: ___
What is still unclear: ___
Placement or scale question: ___
Reference conflict: ___
Likely session shape: ___
Decision needed by the end: ___
```

This keeps the call from spending twenty minutes repeating information the client already submitted.

## Use a simple consultation agenda

### 1. Restate the project

Summarize what you think the client wants. Let them correct the summary before you discuss solutions.

### 2. Clarify the visual direction

Ask what matters most: subject, mood, composition, level of detail, or a specific part of the reference. Explain that references are direction, not a menu for copying another artist's tattoo.

### 3. Test placement and scale

Discuss how the design sits on the body, how it may wrap or move, and whether the requested size supports the detail. For in-person consultations, document any measurements or placement photos with the client's consent.

### 4. Define the project shape

Decide whether it is one session, multiple sessions, or still too open to estimate. For multi-session work, explain what the first session is meant to accomplish without promising an exact finish date you cannot know yet.

### 5. Discuss price and timing honestly

Give a fixed quote only when the scope is fixed enough. Otherwise, give a range, rate, or session-based estimate and state what could change it. Check the client's availability without calling any date booked.

### 6. Set boundaries and open questions

Say which parts of the idea fit your work, which parts need to change, and what you will not take on. Clear boundaries now are kinder than a surprise after a deposit.

### 7. End with a decision

Choose one:

- Ready for approval and date options
- Client needs to send one missing item
- Artist needs time to assess and will reply by a specific date
- Project moves to a later booking window or waitlist
- Project is not a fit

Do not end with "we will see." Give one person one next action.

## Send a written recap

The recap becomes the shared reference for the quote, drawing direction, and deposit step.

```text
Hey ___, thanks for the consultation. Here is the direction we agreed:

Project: ___
Placement and approximate size: ___
Style or visual direction: ___
Session plan: ___
Estimated price or range: ___
What could change the estimate: ___

Next step: ___
Deadline: ___

This is not a confirmed appointment yet. The booking is confirmed after ___
under the policy here: ___.
```

If the direction changes substantially after the recap, review the scope again before taking the deposit. Do not let an old estimate follow a new tattoo.

## Keep consultation notes useful

Store the request, recap, measurements, agreed direction, estimate, and decision together. Separate private artist notes from anything the client can see. Avoid collecting medical or personal details that do not belong in project planning.

For returning clients, add the consultation outcome to the same client record instead of creating a second history. See [keeping tattoo client notes and history](tattoo-client-notes-and-history.md).

## Consultation checklist

- [ ] A structured request arrives before the consultation
- [ ] The consultation format matches the project
- [ ] The client knows what to prepare and whether there is a fee
- [ ] The artist reviews the request and writes the open questions first
- [ ] Visual direction, placement, scale, scope, timing, and price approach are discussed
- [ ] References are treated as direction, not work to copy
- [ ] No appointment is described as confirmed during the consultation
- [ ] The meeting ends with one status and one next action
- [ ] A written recap records what both sides agreed
- [ ] Significant changes trigger a new scope review before deposit
