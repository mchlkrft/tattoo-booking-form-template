# Bad vs Good Booking Request

A side by side look at two booking inquiries for the same artist on the same day. The first is a typical DM that drops in with nothing attached. The second is a structured tattoo request through a proper booking form. Both clients are fictional.

---

## The bad version

> hi! i love your work! how much for a tattoo?

That is the entire message.

What is missing:

- No idea description
- No placement
- No size
- No references
- No dates
- No budget context
- No name beyond the Instagram handle
- No way to know whether the client has even read your work, your style, or your pricing range

To answer that DM properly, the artist now has to send at least five follow-up questions. Each answer arrives separately. The thread becomes a slow back-and-forth that may or may not lead to a booking.

It is not the client's fault. They have probably never seen what a good tattoo request looks like. But it is also not a real request yet.

## The good version

The same client, redirected to the booking form, submits this instead:

> **Name:** Alex
>
> **Instagram handle:** @alex.example
>
> **Email:** alex@example.com
>
> **Tattoo idea:** A small ornamental piece on my inner left forearm. I have been thinking about it for about a year. I would like it to feel hand-drawn and slightly imperfect, not too clean.
>
> **Placement:** Inner left forearm, about 4 cm below the elbow.
>
> **Approximate size:** 8 cm by 6 cm.
>
> **Style direction:** Fineline, ornamental, no color.
>
> **References:** Three images attached. None of them are exact, but they show the direction.
>
> **Existing tattoos near the placement:** A small black line tattoo on the wrist, far enough away that it should not overlap.
>
> **Preferred date window:** Any Thursday or Friday afternoon between March 10 and March 30.
>
> **Approximate budget:** 200 to 300 EUR.
>
> **Deposit policy:** Read and accepted.
>
> **Notes:** This is my second tattoo overall. I tend to get nervous, so a calm session pace works better for me.

The same client. Same project. Completely different starting point.

## Why the second one works

- The artist has enough information to make a real decision in one read.
- References are attached at the time of the request.
- Date flexibility and time of day are clear.
- The budget signals scope without negotiation.
- Personal context (nervousness) is mentioned without becoming the whole message.

The first version takes a week of replies to get to the same place. The second version takes ten minutes from the client and three minutes from the artist.
